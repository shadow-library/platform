Working title: The Lamplighter Twins

I have gone back and forth on this one, so these notes have two versions in them. Version two is what I want now. Version one is only here so I remember what I threw away. Where they disagree, version two wins.

VERSION ONE (old, replaced)

Setting: a canal city called Merrow where the streetlamps are lit by hand every evening by the Lamplighters' Company.

The twins are Cas and Wren Tolley, seventeen. In this version they are enemies from the first page. Cas joins the Lamplighters; Wren joins the Gutter Court, the thieves who want the city dark.

The betrayal happens in the first chapter: Wren sells Cas's route to the Gutter Court and Cas is nearly drowned.

Their mother is alive and runs a chandlery.

The lamps burn ordinary whale oil.

The story is told in first person by Cas.

The villain is the Gutter Court's leader, a woman called Mother Soot.

VERSION TWO (current, use this)

Setting stays the same: Merrow, a canal city, the Lamplighters' Company lights the streetlamps by hand every evening. What changes is the lamps: they burn starwick, a pale oil pressed from a moss that only grows under Merrow's bridges. Starwick light keeps the drowned from climbing out of the canals at night. Nobody in the city says this out loud. Everyone knows it.

The twins are still Cas and Wren Tolley, but they are nineteen now, not seventeen, because I want them to have lived alone for a while.

The big change: Cas and Wren are allies who pretend to be enemies. Both join the Lamplighters' Company. In public they fight, loudly and often, because the Company never lets two members of one family work the same district, and they want to stay together. It is a performance. The reader should be in on it from chapter one.

The betrayal moves. It no longer happens at the start. It happens at the end of book one: Wren does sell a route, but it is a route she believes is safe, and the person she sells it to is not the Gutter Court at all. It is the Company's own Master Lamplighter, who uses it to let the drowned into a district he wants emptied. The betrayal belongs at the end of book one and nowhere earlier.

Their mother is dead. She drowned in the canals when the twins were twelve, the year a lamp went out on Tallow Bridge. The twins have lived alone since.

The story is told in third person, alternating between Cas and Wren chapter by chapter. Not first person any more.

There is no Mother Soot in this version. The villain is the Master Lamplighter, Aurel Pike. He is fifty, charming, and wants to own the starwick harvest. The Gutter Court still exists but it is a side faction, mostly harmless, and mostly children.

How the performance works day to day: Cas takes the east districts and Wren the west, and every evening they meet at the middle of Tallow Bridge to trade insults where the other lamplighters can hear. Then they walk home by separate routes to the same attic above a cooper's shop. The Company's district clerk, a tired man called Oswin Brace, suspects the act and says nothing, because the twins light their streets faster than anyone.

Things that are the same in both versions:
Merrow has one hundred and twelve bridges.
Lamplighters carry a long brass pole with a hook at one end and a wick-cup at the other.
The Company's motto is "No street unlit."
The twins have a signal: two short whistles and one long means "I'm fine, keep fighting me."

Things I have not decided:
Whether the drowned are people who can be saved or something else wearing people's faces.
How book two begins.
